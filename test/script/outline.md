# Nothing To Do — beat outline

**Arc: Kishōtenketsu** (4 movements, turns on a twist rather than conflict).
Chosen over Three-Act because there is no antagonist and inventing one would wreck it:
the obstacle is a flat afternoon and the turn is an idea, not a victory.

**1. KI — Milo arrives.** (~30s, Theo's living room)
Doorbell. Theo yanks the door open mid-sentence, already promising the afternoon.
Milo steps in with his backpack and his expectation. The living room is buried in
toys. Both of them look at all of it.

**2. SHŌ — the long second.** (~30s, same room)
They sit down in the middle of it. Theo offers three things; none of them lands.
Milo says the true thing: *there's nothing to do.* Theo has no answer. The room hums.
This beat has to be allowed to be genuinely flat — if it is charming, the turn is free
and the film is worthless.

**3. TEN — the idea.** (~30s, same room)
Theo's eyes drift, snag on the star-blanket over the couch. He goes quiet — his tell.
He doesn't explain; he just starts pulling cushions. Milo hesitates one beat, then is
all in. They build. The blanket goes up over the top.

**4. KETSU — inside.** (~30s, fort interior)
They dive under. Inside, the printed stars are real stars. Black depth, drifting
nebula, a viewport between two cushions. Weightless. Neither says anything for a long
time. Then Milo, flatly, says the last line — and we pull back to see the fort, small
and glowing, in an ordinary living room.

**Implies:** 4 scenes, ~30s each, **one physical location** (the fort interior is the
same room and is a reveal, not a move). 2 speaking characters. 4 blocks = 300 credits
+ 2 voice casts = 6 runs total.
