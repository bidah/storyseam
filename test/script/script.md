# Nothing To Do

## SCENE 1 — INT. THEO'S LIVING ROOM - LATE AFTERNOON

The door bangs open. THEO, 8, is already talking.

THEO
Okay so I have four ideas and one of them is really good —

MILO, 8, stands on the mat with his backpack still on. He steps in.

Low sun lays a long amber stripe across a rug buried in board games. A navy blanket
printed with pale stars hangs over the back of the teal couch.

Both boys look at all of it.

MILO
Which one's the good one?

Theo opens his mouth. Closes it.

## SCENE 2 — INT. THEO'S LIVING ROOM - CONTINUOUS

They sit down in the middle of the games. Theo lifts a lid, looks in, puts it back.

THEO
We could do this one. Or — that one's missing pieces.

Milo picks up a plastic dinosaur. Sets it down exactly where it was.

MILO
(flat, honest)
There's nothing to do.

Theo doesn't argue. The room hums. Dust turns over in the light.

They sit in it.

## SCENE 3 — INT. THEO'S LIVING ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Theo's eyes drift across the room and stop on the star-blanket.

He goes quiet. Completely quiet.

Then he's moving — hauling cushions off the couch, stacking them into walls. He doesn't
explain any of it.

Milo watches one beat. Two.

Then he's up, dragging cushions, building the other side.

Theo takes the blanket down and throws it. It opens over the top of the fort and settles.

## SCENE 4 — INT. FORT INTERIOR - CONTINUOUS

They crawl in and lie back.

Above them the printed stars are not printed anymore. Black depth. Real light. A slow
nebula drifting past a gap between two cushions.

They don't move. They don't say anything for a long time.

MILO
That's the good one.

We pull back — and it is a blanket fort in an ordinary living room, glowing faintly, in
the last of the afternoon.

FADE OUT.
